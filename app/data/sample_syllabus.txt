PUBLIC ADMINISTRATION
================================================================================

## Paper I — Administrative Theory

### Meaning, Scope and Significance
Public Administration is the study of how government policies are formulated and implemented. Scope includes personnel, finance, organisation, methods, and public relations. Significance: bridge between politics and policy delivery; instrument of socio-economic change in developing societies like India.

### Evolution of Public Administration
- Pre-modern: Kautilya's Arthashastra — espionage, revenue, civil service ethics
- Woodrow Wilson (1887): politics-administration dichotomy — administration as science, politics as choice
- Goodnow: politics makes policy, administration executes; three-fold authority (legislative, executive, judicial)
- Gulick & Urwick POSDCORB: Planning, Organising, Staffing, Directing, Coordinating, Reporting, Budgeting
- New Public Administration (1960s–70s): Waldo, value-neutrality critique, social equity, relevance to developing nations

### Scientific Management and Human Relations
- Frederick Taylor: time-motion studies, piece-rate, functional foremanship, "one best way"
- Henri Fayol: 14 principles — unity of command, scalar chain, esprit de corps
- Max Weber: legal-rational authority, ideal-type bureaucracy — hierarchy, specialization, impersonality, written rules, career system
- Critiques of Weber: Gouldner (ritualism), Merton (goal displacement), Crozier (bureaucratic dysfunction)
- Elton Mayo & Hawthorne experiments: informal groups, social needs, morale affect productivity
- Human Relations School: McGregor Theory X vs Theory Y; Maslow hierarchy of needs in workplace

### Organisational Behaviour and Decision-Making
- Chester Barnard: cooperative systems, zone of indifference, executive functions (provide system, induce participation, sustain morale)
- Herbert Simon: bounded rationality, satisficing (not maximizing), administrative behaviour as decision chains
- Chris Argyris: personality vs organisation conflict; mature employee vs immature organisation design
- Rensis Likert: four systems of management (exploitative → participative)

### Comparative and Development Administration
- Fred Riggs: prismatic society, sala model (fused, diffracted, prismatic); ecology of public administration
- Development administration: goal-oriented, change-agent role, distinction from maintenance administration
- Ferrel Heady: contextual factors — colonial legacy, political culture, level of development

### New Public Management (NPM)
- Origins: UK (Thatcher), NZ reforms, Osborne & Gaebler "Reinventing Government"
- Principles: customer orientation, market mechanisms, performance contracts, agencification, output over input
- Tools: contracting out, PPP, user charges, benchmarking, KPIs, executive agencies
- Criticisms: equity erosion, short-termism, fragmentation, "hollowing out" of state capacity
- India: Second ARC recommendations, mission mode projects, outcome budgeting

### New Public Service and Governance
- Denhardt & Denhardt: serve citizens not customers; democracy in workplace; value people not just productivity
- Governance paradigm: networks, multi-actor, co-production, steering not rowing
- Rhodes: policy networks, differentiated polity, hollow state
- Good governance: World Bank pillars — participation, rule of law, transparency, responsiveness, consensus, equity, effectiveness, accountability

### Accountability and Control
- Types: political (ministerial responsibility), legal (judicial review, tribunals), administrative (hierarchy, audit), social (media, RTI, civil society)
- Legislative control: questions, debates, committees (PAC, Estimates, Department-related)
- Executive control: internal audit, vigilance, performance appraisal
- Judicial control: writ jurisdiction, judicial activism, PIL
- Ombudsman models: Swedish, French Cour des Comptes, India's Lokpal/Lokayukta debate

### Ethics in Administration
- Nolan Committee (UK): selflessness, integrity, objectivity, accountability, openness, honesty, leadership
- Second ARC on ethics: code of ethics, code of conduct, conflict of interest, whistleblower protection
- Indian context: RTI, citizen charters, e-governance for transparency
- Compassionate bureaucracy: welfare delivery, last-mile gaps, empathy in disaster and health administration

## Paper II — Indian Administration

### Constitutional Framework
- Part XIV (Arts 308–323): services under Union and States
- Art 310: doctrine of pleasure; Art 311: safeguards — inquiry, reasonable opportunity
- Art 312: All-India Services; Art 315–323: UPSC and SPSC
- Federal structure: Union List, State List, Concurrent List; administrative federalism

### Evolution of Indian Administration
- Legacy: Indian Civil Service (ICS), Montagu-Chelmsford, Government of India Act 1935
- Sardar Patel and integration of princely states; continuity vs change debate post-1947
- Administrative reforms: Gorwala Committee, Appleby reports, Kothari Committee, Hota Committee

### Union Government
- President: formal head; ordnance-making; pardoning power
- Prime Minister: keystone of cabinet; coordination, policy direction
- Council of Ministers: collective responsibility (Art 75); inner cabinet vs full cabinet
- Cabinet Secretariat: coordination, crisis management, cabinet committees
- Principal Secretary to PM: role in policy prioritisation

### Civil Services
- Recruitment: UPSC — CSE, IFoS, Engineering Services, combined medical; lateral entry debate
- Training: LBSNAA Mussoorie (foundation), phase-wise training, mid-career, foreign training
- Performance: APAR system, 360-degree feedback debates, outcome vs process metrics
- All-India Services: IAS, IPS, IFoS — cadre system, deputation, empanelment
- Central Services: IRS, IFS (Foreign), IAAS, etc.
- Issues: generalists vs specialists, domain expertise, attrition to private sector

### Central Secretariat System
- Ministries vs Departments; attached and subordinate offices
- Secretary as administrative head; desk officer system
- File movement, noting, channel of submission
- PMO: advisory, monitoring, priority projects (PRAGATI)

### Planning and Policy
- Planning Commission (1950–2014): Five-Year Plans, plan federalism, Gadgil formula
- NITI Aayog (2015–): cooperative federalism, competitive federalism, SDGs, Aspirational Districts
- Decentralised planning: district plans, convergence with MPLADS/MLALADS debates

### State Administration
- Governor: constitutional head; discretionary powers
- Chief Minister and Council of Ministers
- Chief Secretary: head of state administration; coordination across departments
- Secretariat at state level; directorates; field formations

### District Administration
- District Collector/DM: revenue, magisterial, developmental roles — historical evolution
- Unified vs specialised district administration debates
- District Planning Committee (Art 243ZD); DRDA, line department coordination
- Crisis role: disasters, elections, law and order

### Local Government
- 73rd Amendment (1992): Panchayati Raj — Gram Sabha, three tiers, Eleventh Schedule, State Election Commission, State Finance Commission
- 74th Amendment: Urban local bodies — municipalities, Twelfth Schedule
- Devolution: functions, funds, functionaries (3F) — uneven across states
- PRI capacity: Sarpanch model, women reservation, PESA (Schedule Areas)

### Public Sector Undertakings
- Rationale: commanding heights, strategic sectors, employment
- Disinvestment policy: strategic sale vs minority stake; CPSE categorisation (Maharatna, Navratna, Miniratna)
- Autonomy packages; Navratna status criteria
- Air India privatisation, LIC IPO — policy shifts

### Law and Order
- Police under State List; IPS cadre; dual control (CM/Home Minister vs DGP)
- Police reforms: National Police Commission, Prakash Singh judgment (2006) — fixed tenure, State Security Commission
- Central agencies: CBI, NIA, NCB — federal tensions
- Community policing, SMART policing, cyber crime cells

### Welfare Administration
- Health: NHM, Ayushman Bharat, primary health centres, ASHA workers
- Education: SSA, RTE Act, mid-day meal, NEP 2020 implementation gaps
- Social justice: SC/ST welfare, disability rights, gender budgeting
- MGNREGA: rights-based welfare, last-mile delivery, DBT linkage

### Financial Administration
- Finance Commission (Art 280): vertical and horizontal devolution
- GST Council: cooperative federalism model
- CAG: audit of receipts and expenditure; PAC follow-up
- FRBM Act: fiscal discipline, escape clauses

## Key Thinkers — Public Administration
- Woodrow Wilson: politics-administration dichotomy
- Max Weber: bureaucracy, authority types (traditional, charismatic, legal-rational)
- Frederick Taylor, Henri Fayol: classical organisation theory
- Elton Mayo: human relations, Hawthorne effect
- Chester Barnard: cooperative systems
- Herbert Simon: bounded rationality, administrative man
- Dwight Waldo: New Public Administration
- Fred Riggs: ecological and prismatic models
- Vincent Ostrom: public choice, polycentric governance
- Osborne & Gaebler: reinventing government

## Contemporary Issues — Public Administration
- E-governance: Digital India, UMANG, DigiLocker, faceless assessment (tax)
- Citizen charters: Sevottam model, quality standards in service delivery
- RTI Act 2005: transparency, Section 8 exemptions, PIO responsibilities
- Administrative reforms: Second ARC (2005–09) — 15 reports, ethics, local governance, conflict resolution
- Cooperative vs competitive federalism: NITI indices, GST, disaster management
- Lateral entry: domain experts in joint secretary roles — debate on merit vs UPSC route
- Mission Karmayogi: iGOT platform, capacity building for civil servants
- AI and governance: predictive policing, algorithmic accountability, data protection (DPDP Act)

HISTORY
================================================================================

## Ancient India
- Indus Valley Civilisation: town planning (Mohenjo-daro, Harappa), trade, script, decline theories (Aryan invasion vs climate)
- Vedic period: Rigvedic vs later Vedic society; varna system origins; janapadas and mahajanapadas
- Buddhism and Jainism: causes of rise, doctrines, Ashoka and spread of Buddhism
- Mauryan Empire: Chandragupta, Kautilya, Ashoka's dhamma, administration
- Gupta period: golden age debates; art, science (Aryabhata), feudalism debate (RS Sharma vs critics)
- South Indian kingdoms: Cholas (local self-government, temple architecture), Pallavas, Cheras

## Medieval India
- Delhi Sultanate: Slave, Khilji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi dynasties; iqta system
- Mughal Empire: Babur to Aurangzeb; mansabdari, jagirdari, zabt (land revenue under Akbar)
- Akbar: sulh-i-kul, ibadat khana, Din-i-Ilahi debates
- Aurangzeb: religious policy, Deccan campaigns, empire fragmentation
- Bhakti and Sufi movements: Kabir, Nanak, Chishti silsila — social reform dimensions
- Vijayanagara and Bahmani kingdoms; Marathas under Shivaji — swarajya, ashtapradhan

## Modern India (1757–1857)
- Decline of Mughals; emergence of regional powers
- British expansion: Plassey (1757), Buxar (1764), subsidiary alliance (Wellesley), doctrine of lapse (Dalhousie)
- Economic impact: deindustrialisation (Dadabhai Naoroji drain theory), land revenue systems (Permanent Settlement, Ryotwari, Mahalwari)
- Revolt of 1857: causes, centres, leaders, nature (mutiny vs first war of independence — Savarkar vs colonial historiography)
- Post-1857: Crown rule, Queen's Proclamation, Indian Councils Acts

## Freedom Struggle (1858–1947)
- Early nationalism: INC founding (1885), Moderates vs Extremists (Surat split 1907)
- Revolutionary movement: Bhagat Singh, HSRA, Ghadar Party
- Gandhian era: Champaran, Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, Quit India; constructive programme
- Muslim League; Lucknow Pact; Khilafat movement
- Constitutional developments: Morley-Minto, Montagu-Chelmsford, Simon Commission, Round Table Conferences, Govt of India Act 1935
- Partition: Cabinet Mission, Mountbatten Plan, Radcliffe line — historiographical debates

## Post-Independence India
- Integration of states; linguistic reorganisation (States Reorganisation Commission 1956)
- Nehruvian era: planning, NAM, Panchsheel, 1962 war
- Green Revolution; bank nationalisation; Emergency (1975–77)
- Liberalisation 1991: Rao-Manmohan reforms
- Historiography: Cambridge, nationalist, Marxist (colonial mode of production), subaltern studies (Ranajit Guha)

## World History (UPSC relevant themes)
- Industrial Revolution: causes, social impact, imperialism link
- French Revolution: causes, phases, legacy for nationalism and rights
- Russian Revolution 1917: Bolsheviks, Lenin, impact on colonies
- World Wars: causes, consequences, Treaty of Versailles, rise of fascism
- Cold War: containment, NAM, decolonisation, collapse of USSR
- UN and post-WWII order; human rights discourse
